Most product process is inherited. Roadmaps, personas, sprints, the split between discovery and delivery: all of it was derived from constraints that mostly no longer hold. This is about re-deriving the practice from what's actually true now, and being honest about which parts survive the rederivation.

## Outline

- What "first principles" actually means here, versus how the phrase gets used as a brag. It's not "ignore what exists," it's "know why it exists before you keep it."
- The inherited assumptions worth putting on trial: the roadmap as a commitment device, the persona as a stand-in for talking to people, the sprint as a unit of anything.
- Rebuild from three questions: who is it for, what changes for them, what's the smallest thing that proves it.
- Why AI makes this urgent rather than optional. When building is cheap, a wrong assumption gets expressed in code faster and costs more to unwind.
- The failure modes: rederiving things that were already correct, and dressing up taste as logic. Both look like rigour and neither is.
- What survives. Some of the inherited process is load-bearing, and saying which parts is more useful than the demolition.
