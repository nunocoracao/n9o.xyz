A chipmaker invests in a model lab, the lab spends it on chips. A cloud provider takes equity in a lab, the lab commits to years of compute. The dollar goes around and gets counted as revenue at every stop. This is about whether that circle is a bubble or just how infrastructure has always been financed, and what test actually tells them apart.

## Outline

- The shapes: vendor financing, equity-for-compute, strategic investment that comes back as booked revenue. Name them concretely before judging them.
- Why this isn't automatically fraud. Railways, telecoms and fibre were all built this way. Circular capital is how you fund a buildout that no single balance sheet can carry.
- Where it turns dangerous: when the circle stops being a bridge to outside demand and becomes the demand.
- The test: what fraction of revenue originates outside the circle, and is that fraction growing? Everything else is narrative.
- What a pop would actually look like versus a slow deflate. The second is more likely and less discussed.
- The angle I can actually speak to: I'm a customer of this buildout, not an investor in it. What changes for people building on top when the money gets tighter.
